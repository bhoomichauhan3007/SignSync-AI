# ISL basics: general reference

This is a general introduction to how sign languages are built. It is not a dictionary and it does not give
the meaning of any ISL sign. A qualified Indian Sign Language teacher must check every statement before it is
used for teaching.

## What an ISL sign is made of

Sign languages, including Indian Sign Language (ISL), build each sign from a few parts that are combined:

1. **Handshape:** the shape the fingers and thumb make. Examples of handshape descriptions are a flat hand,
   a closed fist, a single pointing index finger, or a hand with fingers spread.
2. **Orientation:** the direction the palm and the fingers point (up, down, towards the body, away from it).
3. **Location:** the place where the sign is made, for example in front of the chest, near the face, or on the
   side of the body.
4. **Movement:** how the hand moves: straight, circular, tapping, or wiggling.
5. **Non-manual signals:** facial expression, eyebrows, mouth shape, head and body movement. These can change
   the meaning of a sign or show a question, a negative, or emphasis.

Hand shapes are one part of a sign. Two signs can use the same handshape and still mean different things because
their location, orientation, movement or facial expression is different.

## Fingerspelling and the alphabet

Fingerspelling spells a word one letter at a time with handshapes. It is used for names, places and words that
do not have a sign. The SignSync AI alphabet covers the letters A to Z. Check each letter's handshape with a teacher.

## Numbers

Numbers in ISL are shown with handshapes and sometimes movement. Learn them with a teacher or in a recorded lesson.

## How to start learning Indian Sign Language (ISL): learning advice (general)

- Practise in front of a mirror, or record yourself and compare with a teacher's video.
- Practise short sessions often rather than one long session.
- Learn the handshapes first, then add location, orientation and movement, then facial expression.
- Learn with a certified ISL teacher. Ask the teacher to check your handshapes and facial expression.
- Deaf community members can give you the most natural feedback.

## Emergencies

In India, the national emergency number is 112. In a real emergency, contact emergency services directly.
